Touch Ring Build Sheet

A Seeed XIAO nRF52840 Sense and an MPR121 touch module in a small printed pod, glued to an adjustable steel ring blank. Four copper-tape electrodes under the lid give tap, double tap, hold and swipe. Every part except the pod comes from AliExpress. The circled numbers in the drawings are the item numbers in the parts list.

How it goes together

Drawn to scale at 8 px per mm on a size 10 band (19.8 mm inside). The pod sits on the back of the finger with its long side along the finger; the USB-C end faces the hand. The finger is drawn small enough to leave room for the optional heart-rate sensor, which needs the band about one size larger than you would otherwise wear.

Not from AliExpress: the pod (34 × 25 × 10 mm outside, 1 mm walls and lid, a USB-C slot in one end), printed in resin by JLC3DP or any local printer. The IQS7211E touch chip for the later custom board comes from DigiKey or LCSC, not AliExpress.

Assembly order

  1. Bench first. XIAO on the breadboard; MPR121 by jumpers (3V3, GND, D4, D5, D3, ADDR to 3V3). An I2C scan should show 0x5B. Tape four copper strips to a card and check each one registers.
  2. Print the pod. The lid must be plastic: metal over the electrodes kills the touch signal.
  3. Electrodes: four 6 × 18 mm copper strips on the underside of the lid, 1.5 mm apart. Solder a short 30 AWG lead to the end of each.
  4. Battery: check polarity with the meter, solder red to BAT+ and black to BAT− under the XIAO, then cover the joints and the cell with Kapton. Plug in USB-C; the charge LED lights.
  5. Fit: XIAO on the floor with USB-C in the slot, cell beside it, foam tape over both, MPR121 on the foam, leads to E0–E3, lid on. Tack everything with UV resin.
  6. Glue the pod to the blank's pad, centred on the band, and size the band to your finger.
  7. Optional health: MAX30102 inside the band on the palm side with the NTC beside it, under a thin clear resin window. Run the wires up the side of the band into the pod through a 2 mm hole in the floor beside the pad.
  • The MPR121 and the DRV2605L both default to 0x5A. ADDR to 3V3 fixes it.
  • The XIAO charges at 50 mA by default, so the cell must be at least 50 mAh.
  • Many purple MAX30102 boards pull I2C up to 1.8 V; the XIAO won't read them.
  • Never use a cell without its protection board, and never bend or pierce one.
  • Firmware is the next step: read the MPR121, confirm taps with the IMU, send BLE HID and mcring events.
